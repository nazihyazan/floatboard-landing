Why direct clipboard capture beats saving image files

When gathering references for automotive styling, landscape illustration, or web design, standard browser habits encourage saving every file to disk. After thirty minutes, your Downloads folder contains dozens of generic filenames like photo-1506744038136.jpg, while none of them remain visible where you are actually sketching or coding.

FloatBoard monitors your system clipboard buffer for image data. When you right-click any web photo and choose Copy Image (or press Ctrl + C), the pixels are immediately ingested into your floating working set. The reference stays visible in an always-on-top window beside your editor, eliminating the need to organize temporary files before you make your first design decision.

Step-by-step: Pin web pictures into your active reference set

  1. Open FloatBoard: Launch FloatBoard and ensure the pin icon in the title bar is activated (Always on Top mode).
  2. Browse your reference source: Find high-resolution landscape, nature, or vehicle references on sites like Unsplash or design portals.
  3. Right-click and select 'Copy Image': Do not click 'Copy Image Address' or 'Save Image As'. Choosing 'Copy Image' sends uncompressed pixel data straight to the system clipboard.
  4. Observe instant pin: FloatBoard detects the clipboard event and pins the photo as a clean visual card immediately.
  5. Annotate with notes: Click into FloatBoard's text field to type design constraints, lighting angles, or hex codes beside the photo.

Keep the working set small and purposeful

A high-impact moodboard is not an endless infinite canvas. It is a tight selection of 3 to 6 authoritative benchmarks that guide current work. Keeping fewer images visible forces clarity: one photo for palette, one for silhouette, and one for surface texture.

When an angle or color reference is no longer needed, click its ✕ close icon to discard it. Because FloatBoard works from memory and clipboard buffers, closing a card leaves no orphaned files in your folders.

Frequently asked questions about copying web images

Does copying an image download a file to my hard drive?

No. FloatBoard reads the raster image buffer directly from your operating system's clipboard memory. Your hard drive and downloads folder remain completely clean.

Why did FloatBoard capture a URL instead of a picture?

If you choose 'Copy Link' or 'Copy Image Address', the browser sends plaintext rather than pixel data. Make sure to click 'Copy Image' in your browser's context menu.

Can I drag and drop images directly into FloatBoard?

Yes. Dragging an image file or web element directly into FloatBoard also adds it to your active board immediately.
