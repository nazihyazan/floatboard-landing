Choose the notes needed for the next paragraph

Start with the claim you are about to write. Choose the source detail you must verify, a short quotation if exact wording matters and the question that paragraph should answer. Leave the rest of your research in its original library or project folder.

A useful working note answers four questions: where did this come from, when was it published or checked, what does it actually say, and how will I use it? A floating note is a prompt to check the original source, not a citation by itself.

Copy this source-card structure before drafting

Imagine drafting a short product comparison. The following is a template, not a claim about any specific product:

Claim to check: [one factual statement]
Source: [author or organization + page title + URL]
Published/checked: [date shown by source / date you checked]
Exact wording: "[brief quotation, if needed]"
My paraphrase: [your own wording, clearly labelled]
Where it goes: [draft section or paragraph]
Before publishing: reopen source and verify context

Put the source and exact wording in one labelled note; keep your interpretation separately. If the page has changed, update the note from the original rather than trusting an old clipboard fragment. Do not let a floating note become a lost citation

Use quotation marks for words copied exactly. Keep the original URL, author or organization and relevant publication date with the quote. When you paraphrase, compare your sentence with the full source passage; a short clipped phrase can reverse the meaning of a qualification.

For academic or citation-heavy writing, use a dedicated reference manager. Zotero integrates with word processors and can insert citations and bibliographies. FloatBoard does not replace that source library; it can hold a small visible working set while you consult Zotero or your documents.

If you are working with private correspondence or sensitive research, keep it out of screenshots that you share publicly. Only place material on the working board that is appropriate for that workspace.

Frequently asked questions about visible writing notes

How can I keep notes always on top while writing in Windows?

Open your notes app and use Microsoft PowerToys Always On Top to pin its active window, or use FloatBoard’s own pin control. Resize the note window so Word or your browser stays readable.

Can I keep reference notes beside Google Docs?

Yes. Google Docs runs in a browser window, so you can place a separate notes window beside it or keep a small FloatBoard window pinned above it. If your screen is narrow, a side-by-side layout may work better than an overlapping note.

Should I use a floating note or Zotero for research?

Use Zotero or another reference manager for the source collection and final citations. Use a floating note for the few facts or questions you need in view during the current paragraph. A source card on the board should always point back to the original.

How do I avoid accidentally copying source text into my draft?

Label exact quotations and your own paraphrases differently. Draft from the source idea with the original closed or beside you, then reopen it to verify accuracy and attribution before publishing.
