The temporary screenshot trap: 6 wasted steps per capture

Notice the friction every single time you capture a bug report or UI reference:

  1. You press Win+Shift+S or Cmd+Shift+4.
  2. The OS dumps a 4MB PNG file onto your Desktop.
  3. You double-click the file to open an image viewer.
  4. You click back into your code editor or Figma—and the image viewer immediately vanishes behind your window.
  5. You spend 30 seconds rearranging both windows side by side, cursing your narrow laptop screen.
  6. Three weeks later, you waste 20 minutes deleting 140 obsolete screenshot files to clean up your drive.

That is six tedious steps for a visual reference you only needed for 30 seconds.

Zero files, zero clutter: The floating clipboard loop

With FloatBoard pinned in your screen corner, the loop shrinks to exactly one step: capture.

The moment you clip any region of your screen, FloatBoard's clipboard engine catches the bitmap and renders a crisp visual card in your pinned image panel. It stays floating above your active apps. You glance at it, write your code, verify your layout, and click 'X' when finished. Zero disk clutter, zero desktop mess, zero cleanup days.

In the recorded video demonstration above, we ran a stress test: capturing FloatBoard's own window repeatedly into itself. Each capture instantly nests inside the board, creating an 'infinite mirror' (the classic Droste effect).

Why does this matter beyond the visual spectacle? Because it demonstrates zero latency. There is no cloud round-trip, no thumbnail encoding lag, and no upload progress bar. It is instantaneous, offline screen memory that keeps up with your fastest keyboard shortcuts.

In day-to-day work, engineers and designers use this rapid capture loop to compare progressive UI states side by side: before and after state mutations, responsive mobile vs desktop breakpoints, or sequential frames of micro-interactions without ever leaving their primary app.

Frequently asked questions about floating screenshot references

How do I pin a screenshot so it stays on top of other windows?

Open FloatBoard and ensure the pin icon in the top header is active. Then take a screenshot using your usual keyboard shortcut (such as Win+Shift+S on Windows or Cmd+Shift+4 on Mac). FloatBoard automatically captures the image and keeps it pinned above all other windows.

Does FloatBoard create temporary file clutter on my desktop?

No. FloatBoard manages its working media internally in local application memory and temporary app cache. When you remove a card from the board, it is purged automatically without leaving throwaway files on your desktop.

Can I keep written instructions next to my pinned screenshots?

Yes. FloatBoard features a dual-pane workspace. You can keep terminal commands, hex tokens, or task checklists in the text panel while your reference screenshots remain visible in the adjacent image panel.

Is there a browser-based option to try floating notes?

Yes. You can test the free WebPad online tool directly inside Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge using the Document Picture-in-Picture API without installing anything.
