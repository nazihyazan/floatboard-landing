Set up Front and Side Blueprint Orthographics

Hard-surface modeling in Blender requires constant alignment between front (NumPad 1) and right side (NumPad 3) orthographic projections. Traditionally, artists import reference images as Viewport Empties or Background Images. However, viewport empties can shift when you accidentally rotate your camera, clutter your outliner hierarchy, and slow down viewport playback on complex subdivision surfaces.

FloatBoard decouples your blueprints from the Blender scene. By pinning your front and side reference cards in a hardware-accelerated floating window locked in the upper corner of your monitor, you can freely orbit, pan, and zoom in your perspective viewport (NumPad 5) while keeping the exact silhouette proportions visible.

Avoid Cluttering Blender with Viewport Background Empties

When working on multi-part assets like mechanical props or automotive chassis, keeping image empties inside your Blender file causes several workflow bottlenecks:

  • Empties get accidentally selected during box-select operations in Object Mode unless manually toggled as non-selectable in the Outliner.
  • Depth sorting issues in X-Ray mode (Alt + Z) can obscure mesh wireframes behind semi-transparent references.
  • Exporting FBX or glTF files for Unreal Engine or Unity risks bundling unnecessary reference empties into the game asset hierarchy.

FloatBoard operates completely outside Blender's memory space, ensuring your .blend file remains 100% clean and export-ready.

Check Mesh Proportions and Subdivision Curvature

Before applying a Subdivision Surface modifier, verifying edge loop flow against authentic vehicle or anatomy blueprints prevents tedious retopology later. Placing FloatBoard right above your 3D Viewport lets you align vertices along characteristic silhouette lines with sub-pixel precision.
